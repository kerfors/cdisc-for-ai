Who rates?

One terminology change, four places that carry part of its meaning, and no link between them. A worked example of what flat standards leave implicit, and what an explicit graph would state.

In SDTM Controlled Terminology 2026-09-25 four instrument codelists changed class. Three Clinical Global Impression variants moved from Questionnaire to Clinical Classification, and the Lansky Play-Performance Status Scale moved the other way. Where NCIt states who rates the instrument, each move follows the rater. Yet the rater is not recorded anywhere as data.

The CDISC definition of the Clinical Classification domain (C228234) says these instruments are "based on a trained healthcare professional's observation and clinical judgement". A parent-rated scale does not fit; clinician-rated ones do.

Today and envisioned: the Lansky codelist

Today — four places, no links

Each concept is correct on its own terms. The rater, the class rule and the leftover names from the earlier class sit in text, in different places.

Envisioned — stated and linked

The rater and the class become statements that can be queried and checked. A container still named after the old class, or a parent-rated instrument in Clinical Classification, shows up as a failed check, not as something a reader has to notice.

The same leftovers in all four

Each move renamed the codelists. The question container concept and an old synonym still carry the earlier class.

What the explicit version could look like

An illustration in Turtle. The NCIt identifiers are real; the ex: predicates and classes are placeholders. Choosing them, ideally from established ontologies, is the open work.

@prefix ncit: <http://ncicb.nci.nih.gov/xml/owl/EVS/Thesaurus.owl#> .
@prefix ex:   <https://example.org/illustrative#> .

ncit:C138215  ex:codelistOf     ncit:C38144 .            # LPPSS1TC → Lansky Play-Performance Status
ncit:C38144   ex:ratedBy        ex:Parent ;
              ex:classifiedAs   ex:Questionnaire .         # since SDTM CT 2026-09-25
ncit:C228234  ex:requiresRater  ex:HealthcareProfessional . # Clinical Classification domain
ncit:C138263  ex:containerFor   ncit:C138215 .           # still labelled "Clinical Classification Question"

With statements like these, two questions become queries: which instruments are clinician-rated? and does any instrument's class contradict its rater, or its container? A change note can then say what a reclassification means — the rater did not fit the class — and not only which labels changed.
